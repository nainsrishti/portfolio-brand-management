My Brand Philosophy

That realization changed how I see my work. Brands aren't just logos and taglines — they're ecosystems of trust. And trust either compounds or collapses depending on how a brand shows up, again and again.

I became obsessed with understanding what makes people believe a brand, what makes them stay loyal to it, and what quietly breaks that trust. Not just reach and impressions — the actual human loops underneath brand loyalty.

I build for belonging, but I design for loyalty. I care about the quality of every touchpoint, the consistency of brand voice, and the subtle rituals that make a brand feel like part of someone's life — not just a name they recognize.

My Creative Side

I've been drawn to making things since childhood — art, writing, poetry. Creativity has always been how I think, not just how I express.

My digital art journey started before college. I noticed friends creating illustrations on Twitter and jumped in. What started as curiosity became a practice — and that practice taught me that the best work comes from genuine obsession, not obligation.

Today I write Urdu poetry, sketch, doodle, and 3D print when I'm not building brands. It keeps me grounded and honest about what it means to make something people actually care about — the same instinct behind every brand story I build.

Education & Learning

It started formally with psychology. I pursued my Bachelor's in Applied Psychology thinking I'd study behavior through theory. But it was the student council, not Skinner, that taught me what really drives people.

College gave me more than a degree — it gave me drive. I ran competitions, won a few, led the Business Club, gave seminars, joined hackathons, and somewhere in between all that, I started dreaming bigger.

Then the campus gates opened, and the real world hit different. My real learning started not in lecture halls but in communities, events, and late-night problem-solving sessions. I picked up JS, AI tools, Figma, and Notion — anything that helped me bring ideas to life faster.

The Applied Psychology degree wasn't just academic background. It's the lens I use every single day — to understand why people believe in a brand, what breaks that trust, and how to design brand experiences around real human behaviour, not just behaviour patterns.

Professional Experience

I've been working since my first year of college — content writing, social media, freelance. By my third year I was doing offline internships and building real things with real stakes.

My first proper role was Community Lead at Oopar Club in Bangalore — a pre-seed funded startup solving urban loneliness. I hosted events, built member relationships, and created the Social Score — a psychometric tool that screened and onboarded new members for personality fit, protecting the brand's promise before a single person walked through the door. That's where I understood what it takes to build brand trust from the inside out.

Then came IMT Alumni Association — a 40-year-old registered brand representing an estimated 30,000+ MBAs, CEOs, CXOs, and founders. I joined as the sole employee. No team, no playbook, just full ownership of everything — content, brand voice, events, membership, WhatsApp groups across 50+ communities, a strategic partnership with IIMJobs, and building an Angel Network from scratch.

That's where I grew up professionally. And it's the kind of work I want to keep doing — building brands that outlast the people who started them.

Values & Vision

I believe the best brands are ones people stop thinking of as brands — because it just feels like their people, their voice. That's the standard I build toward.

Inclusion isn't a feature for me, it's the foundation. A brand that only speaks to one type of person isn't really a brand — it's a clique. I care about building brand voices that people from different backgrounds, cities, and career stages all feel spoken to by.

My goal is to keep building brands that create real value — not just reach numbers, but actual trust, loyalty, and growth for the people who show up for them. And eventually, to lead organisations that treat brand as the core product, not an afterthought.
